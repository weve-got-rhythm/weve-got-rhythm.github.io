PROGRAMS

We've Got Rhythm bridges the gap, one instrument and one ticket at a time.

PHASE I: ACCESS

BEATS

Bringing Equipment & Access To Students

For many young people, access to music education begins with a simple question: Do they have an instrument of their own?

For families balancing essential expenses, instrument rental fees or purchase costs create an immediate barrier. Without an instrument at home, practice is limited, progress slows, and a young person may never discover how far their musical interest could take them.

Through BEATS, we curate worthy, well-loved instruments and find new musical homes for them. We place quality instruments directly into the hands of aspiring students and partner public school classrooms at zero cost, so that practice never has to wait.

Owning an instrument changes a young person's relationship with music. It gives them the freedom to practice on their own time, build confidence through repetition, and see music as something that truly belongs to them.

PHASE II: CONNECTION

The Community Gateway

Access works best when it begins with trust.

Schools, families, and community organizations help us understand where barriers exist and where an opportunity can make the greatest difference. These relationships allow WGR to reach young people where they already learn and grow, connect them with the right resources, and extend opportunities beyond what may be available through school or family resources alone.

The goal is not simply to offer a program. It is to create a reliable point of entry into experiences, resources, and relationships that might otherwise remain out of reach.

PHASE III: POSSIBILITY

#RhythmSupport

A world-class performance should not feel like a world away.

Through #RhythmSupport, we provide young people with complimentary concert access, include parents and guardians when possible, help with transportation when needed, and create opportunities to meet performing artists backstage.

The experience is about more than attending a concert. Seeing extraordinary musicians perform up close, entering renowned cultural spaces, and speaking directly with artists can make excellence feel more human, more accessible, and more imaginable.
